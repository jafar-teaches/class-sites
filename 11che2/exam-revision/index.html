How to use this page

Spread your revision over three to four weeks, in this order. Each step tells you what to find out about yourself, so the next step goes to your weakest topics.

  1. Week 1

    Check

    Open each topic card below and tick only the "I can" statements you can do now, without notes.

    Pick your three weakest topics. Do the Easy and Medium worksheet questions for them.

  2. Week 2

    Practise by topic

    Work through the revision worksheet one topic at a time, including the Hard questions.

    Mark each topic with the worked solutions before you move on. Then sit that topic's test (46 minutes) and do the quick-check quiz.

  3. Week 3

    Sit the practice exam

    Sit it in one go under exam conditions: 10 minutes reading, 90 minutes writing, with only the Data Book and a calculator.

    Mark it with the marking guide. Write the topic beside every mark you lost.

  4. Final week

    Fix weak topics

    Go back to the topics where you lost marks. Redo those worksheet questions without looking at your first attempt.

    Retake the quiz, then reread the exam technique tips.

About the practice exam. It has the same two sections as the real exam but is slightly longer (78 marks), so finishing it in 90 minutes is good pacing practice.

Exam technique

General habits that earn marks in any VCE Chemistry exam.

Use reading time to plan

You can't write during the 10 minutes of reading time, so read Section B first. Decide which questions you will start with and note what each one asks for.

Pace yourself by the marks

Allow a little over a minute per mark: about 25 minutes for Section A and 65 minutes for Section B. If a part is taking too long, move on and come back.

Show every step of working

Write the formula, substitute the values with units, then give the answer. Set out one step per line, because marks are given for correct steps even if the final answer is wrong.

Units and significant figures

  • Give every final answer a unit.
  • Match the significant figures to the least precise value in the data.
  • Keep extra digits during the working and round once, at the end.
  • Energy released or absorbed is a positive value, such as 12.3 kJ.

Know your Data Book

Before the exam, find these quickly: the periodic table with molar masses, SLC and the molar volume of a gas, the gas constant R, the specific heat capacity of water and the electrochemical series. Use the Data Book values, not ones you remember.

Equations and gas calculations

  • Balance atoms and charge, and give states in every equation.
  • In acidic conditions, balance half-equations with water, H₂O, hydrogen ions, H⁺, and electrons.
  • For pV = nRT, convert to kPa, L and K before you substitute.

Answer what the question asks

"State" or "identify" needs one precise point. "Explain" needs a reason and a link back to the situation in the question. If it asks for a mass, give a mass, not an amount in mol.

Multiple choice

Answer every question, because there is no penalty for a wrong answer. For calculations, work out your answer before you look at the options, since wrong options often match a common slip.
