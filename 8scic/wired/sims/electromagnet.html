Where this model simplifies
  • The current here is much bigger than a small battery could give. Real compasses near a real wire move less, and only when they sit close to it.
  • Each needle is also pulled by Earth's magnetic field, so the needles do not make a perfect circle.
  • Field lines are a model. The real field is everywhere around the wire, not only on the lines.
  • In the real demonstration, a resistor in the circuit keeps the current safe, and the current is on for a few seconds only.

Where this model simplifies
  • The simulation's clip numbers come from a simple rule and are bigger than a real nail gives. Predict the shape of the graph, not the numbers. Your Practical 3 results will also be messier.
  • In this model, the number of paperclips levels off as the magnet gets very strong. The iron core can only be magnetised up to a limit.
  • A real iron nail keeps a little magnetism after you switch off, so one or two clips may stay stuck. Here they all drop.
  • In a real circuit, more turns means a longer wire with more resistance, so the current drops a little. Here the current stays exactly where you set it.
  • A real coil gets warm. Always switch off between trials.
