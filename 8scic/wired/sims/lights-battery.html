Where this model simplifies
  • Each bulb has the same fixed resistance of 40 Ω (ohms). A real bulb's resistance changes as it heats up. Dim bulbs stay cooler, so they would take a little more current than shown.
  • Kai's real lights are LED lights. An LED light would not light at all on only 4 V, so we treat them as simple bulbs here.
  • The battery bank is really 12.8 V. We use 12 V to keep the numbers simple.
  • The battery stays at 12 V until it is flat. A real battery's voltage drops slowly as it runs down, and the wires have a little resistance too.
  • Only the lights are switched on. The fridge, pump and laptop are left out.
  • The simulation's lights are 40 Ω (0.30 A each). Real bulbs differ, but the pattern is the same.
