Extension

Electrons carry the charge in metal wires. They drift the other way, from − to +. The small bars just outside the wire show them. Real electrons drift very slowly, about 0.1 mm each second.

Where this model simplifies
  • Each bulb's resistance here never changes. A real bulb lets less current through once its filament gets hot.
  • Each cell here always gives exactly 1.5 V. A real cell gives a little less when current flows, and less again as it goes flat.
  • The dots are huge and far apart. A real wire holds billions of billions of charges.
  • In metal wires, electrons really carry the charge. They drift from − to +, at about 0.1 mm each second.
  • The light comes on straight away, because the whole loop of charge starts moving at once. The dots here also start together.
  • The orange fill is a picture to help you. Scientists describe how the energy travels in a more detailed way.
