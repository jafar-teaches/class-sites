Worked example

Working backwards from efficiency

Sometimes you know the useful energy out and the efficiency. Then you work backwards to find the total energy in.

The problemA phone charger is 80% efficient. To charge a phone, 36,000 J of useful energy must be stored in its battery. How much electrical energy does the charger take from the wall?
  1. Write what you know.Useful energy out = 36,000 J. Efficiency = 80% = 0.80
    Change the percentage to a decimal by dividing by 100.
  2. Rearrange the formula.Total energy in = useful energy outefficiency
    Useful out = efficiency × energy in. To undo the ×, divide by the efficiency.
  3. Substitute.Total energy in = 36,0000.80
  4. Answer with a unit.Total energy in = 45,000 J
  5. Check it makes sense.45,000 J is bigger than 36,000 J, so it passes.
    Some energy is always wasted. So the energy in is always bigger than the useful energy out.

Common slip: 36,000 × 0.80 = 28,800 J. That is smaller than the useful energy, which is impossible. If your energy in comes out smaller, you multiplied instead of divided.

You know the energy in.Find the useful energy out by multiplying: energy in × efficiency.
You know the useful energy out.Find the energy in by dividing: useful outefficiency.

Now try three yourself

Write your working on paper first. Then check it one question at a time.

Easy

An electric scooter's motor is 90% efficient. To get up to speed, the scooter needs 5,400 J of kinetic energy. How much electrical energy does the battery give the motor?

  1. Useful energy out = 5,400 J. Efficiency = 90% = 0.90
  2. Energy in = useful outefficiency = 5,4000.90
  3. Energy in = 6,000 J
  4. Check: 6,000 J is bigger than 5,400 J.
Medium: multiply or divide?

A small hydro power station is 85% efficient. Each second, falling water gives it 4,000,000 J of kinetic energy. How much electrical energy does it produce each second?

  1. This time you know the energy in (4,000,000 J), so multiply.
  2. Useful energy out = energy in × efficiency = 4,000,000 × 0.85
  3. Useful energy out = 3,400,000 J each second
  4. Check: 3,400,000 J is smaller than 4,000,000 J. Useful energy out is always smaller than energy in.
Hard

A blender is 70% efficient. In one use, it loses 2,700 J to heat and sound. How much electrical energy did it take in?

  1. 70% is useful, so 100% − 70% = 30% is wasted. 30% = 0.30
  2. The wasted energy is 30% of the energy in, so energy in = 2,7000.30
  3. Energy in = 9,000 J
  4. Check: useful out = 9,000 × 0.70 = 6,300 J, and 6,300 + 2,700 = 9,000 J.
