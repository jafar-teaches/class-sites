The whole chapter

The big ideas

  • Energy can move from place to place. Heat moves by conduction, convection and radiation.
  • A wave carries energy from one place to another. The particles stay in place.
  • Sound is a wave that needs a medium. Light is a wave that does not need a medium.
  • Your ears and eyes change wave energy into electrical signals for your brain.
  • Doctors use sound waves to see inside the body.

Lesson 1

Heat transfer

  • Heat always moves from hot to cold.
  • Conduction is heat moving through a solid, from particle to particle.
  • Convection is heat moving when a hot liquid or gas rises, cools and sinks. This makes a loop called a convection current.
  • Radiation is heat moving as invisible waves. It does not need particles, so it can cross empty space.

Lesson 2

Waves

  • A wave carries energy. The particles stay in place.
  • The medium is the stuff a wave travels through, such as air, water or rock.
  • In a transverse wave, the particles move across the way the wave moves (up and down).
  • In a longitudinal wave, the particles move along the way the wave moves (back and forth). Squashed together is a compression. Spread out is a rarefaction.
  • Mechanical waves, like sound, need a medium. Electromagnetic waves, like light, do not.

Lesson 3

Features of waves

  • The amplitude is the height from the rest line to a crest. A bigger amplitude carries more energy.
  • The wavelength (λ) is the distance from one crest to the next crest. It is measured in metres (m).
  • The frequency (f) is how many waves pass a point each second. It is measured in hertz (Hz).
  • A higher frequency makes the wavelength shorter.

Lesson 4

Sound energy

Press Play. Then try the other buttons.

  • Sound is energy carried by vibrating particles. A vibration is a fast back and forth movement.
  • Sound is a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions.
  • Volume is how loud a sound is. It depends on the amplitude. It is measured in decibels (dB).
  • Pitch is how high or low a sound is. It depends on the frequency.
  • Sound is fastest in solids and slowest in gases. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum (empty space).

Lesson 5

The Doppler effect

  • The Doppler effect is a change in the frequency you hear when the source of a sound moves towards you or away from you.
  • The source is the thing that makes the sound. The observer is the person who hears it.
  • Moving towards you: you hear a higher pitch. Moving away from you: you hear a lower pitch.
  • The source itself never changes its note.
  • Police radar guns use the Doppler effect to measure the speed of cars.

Lesson 6

How we hear

  • People can hear sounds from about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz. Lower is infrasound. Higher is ultrasound.
  • The ear has three sections: the outer ear (pinna and ear canal), the middle ear (eardrum and ossicles) and the inner ear (cochlea).
  • The ossicles are three tiny bones. They make the vibrations stronger.
  • Hair cells in the cochlea change the vibrations into electrical signals. The auditory nerve carries them to the brain.
  • Very loud sounds damage the hair cells. They do not grow back. Earmuffs and earplugs protect your ears.

Lesson 7

First Nations peoples’ use of sound

The wobbly part shows what vibrates in each instrument.

  • An idiophone vibrates itself when it is hit or shaken, such as clapsticks.
  • A membranophone has a stretched sheet that vibrates, such as a drum.
  • An aerophone makes the air vibrate, such as the didjeridu.
  • A longer tube makes a lower frequency. It sounds deeper.
  • Knowledge of how to make and play instruments has been passed on over many generations. Each Nation has its own instruments, names and ways of doing things.

Lesson 8

The electromagnetic spectrum

  • The electromagnetic spectrum has seven kinds of waves, in order of wavelength.
  • Shorter wavelength means higher frequency and more energy.
  • All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum: about 300 000 km/s.
  • UV, X-rays and gamma rays carry a lot of energy. They can damage cells, so we need to take care with them.

Lesson 9

Light waves

  • Reflection is when light bounces off a surface. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. Both are measured from the normal.
  • Refraction is when light bends as it goes from one material into another.
  • Light can also be absorbed (taken in) or scattered (sent off in many directions).
  • A convex lens brings rays together. A concave lens spreads rays apart.

Lesson 10

How we see

  • Light comes into the eye from the object. Nothing comes out of the eye.
  • The iris makes the pupil bigger in dim light and smaller in bright light.
  • The cornea and the lens bend light to focus it. The lens changes shape for near and far objects.
  • The retina changes light into electrical signals. The optic nerve carries them to the brain.
  • Rods work in dim light and see grey. Cones need bright light and see colour.

Lesson 11

Medical uses of sound

  • Ultrasound is sound above 20 000 Hz. It is too high for humans to hear.
  • A probe sends out ultrasound. Echoes bounce back from inside the body. A computer uses them to make a picture.
  • Ultrasound carries low energy, so it is safe for scanning an unborn baby.
  • Diagnostic uses find out what is wrong. Therapeutic uses treat or heal.
