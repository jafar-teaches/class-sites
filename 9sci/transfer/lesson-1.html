Heat transfer: conduction, convection and radiation

Learning goal

I will be able to describe how thermal energy is transferred using the particle model.

  • MustDefine conduction, convection and radiation.
  • ShouldGive an example of each method, and explain the role of particles in each one.
  • CouldWork out which method mostly transfers heat in solids, liquids and gases.

Good Science 9, section 9.1.

Warm up

Think of three times you have felt heat. For example, the Sun on your skin, a warm mug in your hands, or the air near a heater.

For each one, guess how the heat reached you. Then share your three with the person next to you.

In your book, write your three examples. Next to each, write one word for how you think the heat travelled.

Suggested answer

Any three real examples are fine. Some common ones:

  • Energy from the Sun travels through space to your skin, so it is radiation.
  • A warm mug heats your hands by touch, so it is conduction.
  • Warm air from a heater moves around the room, so it is convection.

Don't worry if you weren't sure. These three words are what this lesson is about.

Demo predictions

Your teacher will show you three demonstrations. Before each one, choose what you think will happen.

Demo APins on a metal rod

Pins are stuck along a metal rod with wax. One end of the rod is heated.

Which pin will fall off first?

Prediction chosen. Now watch the demo.

What happened

The pin nearest the heat fell first, then the next pin, and so on along the rod.

Energy moved along the rod from the hot end towards the cold end. This is conduction.

Demo BPurple crystal in water

A purple crystal sits at the bottom of a beaker of water. The beaker is heated under one side only.

Where will the purple colour go?

Prediction chosen. Now watch the demo.

What happened

The purple water rose above the heat, moved across the top, then sank down the other side. It moved in a loop.

The heated water itself moved and carried the energy. This is convection.

Demo CHand beside a lamp

A volunteer holds their hand beside a hot lamp, not above it and not touching it.

Will they feel any warmth?

Prediction chosen. Now watch the demo.

What happened

Yes, the hand felt warm from the side. The energy travelled straight out from the lamp in every direction.

It did not need to touch anything or move any air. This is radiation.

Three ways heat moves

Heat is thermal energy moving from a hotter place to a colder place. On its own, it always moves from hot to cold.

Thermal energy can move in three ways. Choose a tab, then press the button under the picture.

Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through a material by particles bumping into the particles next to them.
  1. 1Heat makes the particles at the hot end vibrate faster.
  2. 2They bump into the particles next to them, so those particles vibrate faster too.
  3. 3The particles stay in place. Only the energy moves along the rod.

Look: the particles near the heat wobble more and turn red first. Blue means slow vibration and red means fast vibration.

Conduction works best in solids, because their particles are packed close together. Metals are the best conductors.

Convection is the transfer of thermal energy by a liquid or gas moving from one place to another.
  1. 1Heated water expands. It becomes less dense (the same amount takes up more space), so it rises.
  2. 2At the top it cools, becomes denser and sinks back down.
  3. 3This loop of rising and sinking is called a convection current.

Look: red particles rise above the heat on the left. They turn blue as they cool and sink on the right.

Convection only happens in liquids and gases, because their particles are free to move around.

Radiation is the transfer of thermal energy by infrared waves. Infrared is a type of wave like light, but our eyes can't see it.
  1. 1All objects give off infrared radiation. Hotter objects give off more.
  2. 2The waves travel in straight lines and do not need particles. They can cross empty space.
  3. 3When the waves hit an object, the object absorbs some of the energy and warms up.

Look: the waves cross the gap where there are no particles, and the can's temperature rises.

Dark, dull surfaces absorb and give off radiation well. Light, shiny surfaces reflect it.

What the pictures leave out
  • Real particles are far too small to see, and there are far more of them.
  • In metals, tiny charged particles called free electrons also carry energy. That is why metals conduct so well.
  • Real convection currents are messier than one neat loop.

Practice questions

Write your answers in your book. Check each one with the suggested answer when you're done.

Must

1. Define conduction, convection and radiation in one sentence each.

Suggested answer
  • Conduction is the transfer of thermal energy through a material by particles bumping into the particles next to them.
  • Convection is the transfer of thermal energy by a liquid or gas moving from one place to another.
  • Radiation is the transfer of thermal energy by infrared waves, which do not need particles.

Must

2. A metal spoon is left in a bowl of hot soup. After a minute, the end of the handle feels hot. Name the method of heat transfer, and explain how the energy reached the handle.

Suggested answer

Conduction. The particles in the spoon near the soup vibrate faster. They bump into the particles next to them, passing the energy along the spoon to the handle.

Should

3. Saucepans often have plastic or wooden handles. Explain why.

Suggested answer

Plastic and wood are poor conductors (insulators). Very little thermal energy is conducted through them, so the handle stays cool enough to hold.

Should

4. A room heater is usually placed low down, near the floor. Use the idea of a convection current to explain how it heats the whole room.

Hint

Think about what happens to the air right next to the heater. Is it more dense or less dense than the cooler air?

Suggested answer

Air next to the heater warms up, becomes less dense and rises. The warm air spreads across the ceiling, cools, becomes denser and sinks on the far side. Cool air along the floor flows back to the heater to be warmed again. This loop is a convection current, and it carries thermal energy around the whole room.

Should

5. A tiled floor and a carpet are in the same room, so they are the same temperature. In bare feet, the tiles feel much colder. Explain why.

Hint

Cold doesn't flow into your foot. Think about which way thermal energy moves, and which material conducts better.

Suggested answer

Your foot is warmer than both, so thermal energy moves out of your foot. Tiles are better conductors than carpet, so they carry energy away from your foot faster. Your foot loses heat quickly, so the tiles feel colder.

Could

6. Which method mostly transfers heat in a solid, in a liquid and in a gas? Use the particle model to explain your answer.

Hint

Think about how close together the particles are, and whether they can move from place to place.

Suggested answer
  • Solid: conduction. The particles are packed closely and held in place, so they can pass vibrations on but cannot move around.
  • Liquid and gas: convection. The particles can move from place to place, so warm parts of the liquid or gas rise and carry energy.
  • Radiation can pass through air, some liquids and glass, and also through empty space.

Could

7. Emergency blankets are thin, shiny sheets wrapped around people who are very cold. Explain how the shiny surface helps keep the person warm.

Suggested answer

The person's body gives off infrared radiation. The shiny surface reflects most of this radiation back towards the body, so less thermal energy is lost.

Goal check

How does the Sun's energy reach Earth? Which method of heat transfer is it?

Between the Sun and Earth there is mostly empty space, with almost no particles. Use this in your answer.

Write two sentences in your book to answer the question.

Suggested answer

The Sun's energy reaches Earth by radiation, as light and infrared waves. Conduction and convection both need particles, but radiation does not, so it is the only method that can cross empty space.
